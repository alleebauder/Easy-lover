# Sources and research notes

This file records what the suite's structure is based on, which sources were
checked, where they disagree, and what is still unverified. Prompts and option
wording in `modules.js` are original; this file covers the structure only.

## Structure of the original program

Verified across several sources:

- The original has **four** programs: Past Authoring, Present Authoring (Faults),
  Present Authoring (Virtues), and Future Authoring. Some secondary sites describe
  three modules by counting Present as one. This suite follows the four-program version.
- **Past Authoring** divides life into epochs and works through significant
  experiences in each, examining their impact and what they taught.
- **Present Authoring** (Faults and Virtues) is organized around the **Big Five**
  personality dimensions: openness, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness,
  and emotional stability. Each dimension has a checklist; the user chooses the traits
  that apply, narrows them to the most costly (or strongest), and writes about them.
- **Future Authoring** asks for an ideal future three to five years out, starts with
  a free-write (about 15 to 20 minutes), then covers life areas, and refines goals:
  ranking them, examining why each matters, its effect on self and others, plans,
  obstacles, and progress tracking.
- Peterson's own site recommends spreading each program over several sessions,
  about four to five hours in total.

## Research the design draws on

- **Expressive writing**: James Pennebaker (University of Texas at Austin), on
  structured writing about emotional experiences and health.
- **Goal planning**: Gary Latham (Rotman School of Management, University of Toronto),
  on personal planning and productivity.
- **Future writing**: Laura King's studies on writing about future goals and
  positive experiences.
- Peterson's account of why unresolved memories keep intruding, and the claim that
  writing about them can reduce that vigilance. This comes from secondary summaries
  of his talks, not from a peer-reviewed study.

## Discrepancies between sources

| Topic | What sources say | What the suite does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Number of Past epochs | One guide says six to ten; another says seven. | Prompts for six to ten. |
| Life areas in Future | A podcast summary lists seven: intimacy, family, friendship, career, learning, self-care, community. A different summary lists people admired, improvements, education and career, habits, family, social network, and leisure. | Uses the seven areas, and adds a separate "people I admire" prompt. |
| Life-domain count | A video title reads "Six Dimensions of Your Life," while summaries list seven. | Follows the seven-area version. Unverified. |
| Number of Present programs | Some sites count three programs; the official site lists four. | Uses four (Past, Faults, Virtues, Future), plus the Plan. |
| Recommended order | Sources differ. Past first is common; some suggest Faults first to sharpen honesty. | Presents the order as flexible, with Past as the natural start. |
| Faults and Virtues item count | Summaries give 2 to 10 picks per dimension and 6 to 9 faults to write about. | Allows up to four picks per dimension and three ranked items. |

## Sources checked and not relied on

- Several "free PDF" pages and content farms repeat generic, keyword-heavy
  descriptions of the program with claims that do not match the official site
  (for example, invented co-authors or theories). Excluded.
- A copy of the official instructions hosted on a student-document site was used
  only to confirm the Faults and Virtues structure. Its exact time and item-count
  figures are not verified against the official site.

## Not verified

- **The YouTube explainers and the Joe Rogan #877 episode** could not be read in
  full from this session; only titles and descriptions were available. The "How the
  Self Authoring Program Works" video and the Rogan episode may contain details not
  reflected here. If you send transcripts or notes, the structure can be checked
  against them.
- **Official item lists** for the Faults and Virtues checklists are proprietary. The
  checklist options in `modules.js` were written from the public Big Five dimensions,
  not copied from the official lists.
- **Time estimates.** The module times are estimates based on the four-to-five-hour
  guidance. Individual experience will vary.

## Wellbeing

Past and Faults work involve difficult memories and self-criticism. Sources describe
the faults section as confronting and suggest doing it when you feel steady. The
suite follows that advice in its module notes and shows a crisis line on every page.
